## Liquidity Cycles and Yen Carry Trade Analysis

The analysis of liquidity cycles reveals distinct phases that directly impact hedge fund positioning and strategy implementation. Understanding these cycles, particularly in the context of the unwinding 40-year yen carry trade, provides crucial insights for timing market entries and exits.

![Figure: Instructor beginning analysis of liquidity cycles and their market impact](frame:scene_0057.jpg)

The yen carry trade represents one of the most significant structural forces in global markets, originating from the 1985 Plaza Accord. This agreement fundamentally altered the relationship between Japan and global capital markets, creating a 40-year arbitrage opportunity that is now unwinding with dramatic market implications.

![Figure: Chart analysis showing the historical development of yen carry trade dynamics](frame:periodic_0147.jpg)

The Plaza Accord established a framework where Japan was forced to maintain near-zero interest rates in exchange for continued access to US markets. This created an unprecedented opportunity for global investors to borrow Japanese yen at virtually no cost and deploy that capital in higher-yielding markets worldwide, including the US, India, and emerging markets.

![Figure: Discussion of the Plaza Accord's impact on global capital flows](frame:scene_0061.jpg)

The mechanics of the yen carry trade required currency stability between the yen and deployment currencies. As long as the yen remained stable or weakened relative to other currencies, investors could capture the interest rate differential with minimal currency risk. This trade became so embedded in global finance that it influenced capital allocation decisions across all major markets.

![Figure: Technical analysis showing yen carry trade flow patterns](frame:periodic_0152.jpg)

The unwinding of this trade began approximately 1.5 years before this session when the Japanese government started raising interest rates for the first time in decades. This policy reversal has created significant volatility as positions built over 40 years are systematically closed, affecting global liquidity and market stability.

![Figure: Market data illustrating the impact of yen carry trade unwinding](frame:periodic_0155.jpg)

The comparison between 1985 Japan and 2025 China reveals important structural differences that prevent China from replicating Japan's role in global carry trades. While both countries had export competitiveness with the US as their primary market, China maintains capital controls that prevent the free capital flow necessary for carry trade dynamics.

![Figure: Instructor explaining the differences between Japan 1985 and China 2025](frame:scene_0063.jpg)

China's capital control restrictions create a fundamentally different dynamic than Japan's free capital flow environment. Despite Chinese investors' ability to purchase property in Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, and other markets through complex structures, the systematic capital flow required for carry trades cannot develop under current Chinese regulations.

![Figure: Analysis of Chinese capital controls and their market implications](frame:scene_0064.jpg)

The liquidity phases can be clearly identified through market behavior and central bank actions. Phase 1 saw liquidity double from 400 to 800 units, creating the massive asset price appreciation observed from 2020-2021. Phase 2 represents government attempts to stabilize rather than aggressively withdraw liquidity, creating the sideways market action from 2022-2025.

![Figure: Chart showing distinct liquidity phases and their market characteristics](frame:periodic_0163.jpg)

The current phase represents a gradual liquidity withdrawal as governments attempt to manage inflation without triggering deflationary collapse. This delicate balance explains the increased market volatility and the breakdown of traditional asset class correlations as all assets respond to liquidity changes rather than fundamental factors.
