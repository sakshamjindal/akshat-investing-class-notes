# 04 Us Stocks Feb2025 Part2

*This comprehensive hedge fund strategy document examines the integration of fundamental and technical analysis for US stock selection, emphasizing business value assessment as the foundation before applying technical timing. The material covers systematic position building methodologies using margin of safety calculations, staged capital deployment strategies, and risk management frameworks designed to protect downside while capturing upside potential in equity markets.*

## Fundamental & Technical Analysis Integration

The cornerstone of successful hedge fund strategy lies in the disciplined integration of fundamental business analysis with technical chart patterns for stock selection. This approach emphasizes business value as the foundational element before applying any technical timing considerations.

![Figure: AMD stock chart showing multi-year price action with trend analysis](frame:scene_0001.jpg)

The fundamental-first, technical-second approach requires a systematic entry process that begins with rigorous fundamental analysis to determine whether a business will become more valuable over time. Only after passing this fundamental screening should technical analysis be applied to time entry points and manage position building. As the instructor emphasizes: "If the business, for example, on all these stocks, if you don't see these businesses becoming more valuable with time, then don't do any trend analysis."

![Figure: Technical analysis chart displaying trend lines and support levels](frame:periodic_0003.jpg)

This methodology recognizes that trends can reverse rapidly before meaningful positions can be built, making fundamental conviction the essential foundation for holding through volatility. Technical analysis serves as a tactical tool rather than a strategic decision-maker. The instructor notes: "Trend is very good if you already trust the stock."

![Figure: Market analysis showing price movements and technical indicators](frame:scene_0002.jpg)

### Segment-Level Analysis Framework

Understanding revenue drivers by business segment is critical to identifying growth catalysts and headwinds. This requires deep-dive analysis into each revenue segment of target companies, tracking year-over-year growth rates and investigating anomalies such as significant declines or outperformance.

![Figure: Financial data presentation showing segment performance metrics](frame:periodic_0006.jpg)

The AMD case study exemplifies this approach. The company's Data Center segment showed remarkable +69% year-over-year growth, representing a strong growth driver, while the Gaming segment declined -59% year-over-year, creating a major headwind requiring investigation. The root cause analysis revealed that the gaming decline was due to console inventory clearance by Microsoft and Sony, representing a temporary rather than structural issue.

![Figure: AMD business segment breakdown and performance analysis](frame:scene_0003.jpg)

Product lifecycle and inventory management significantly impact near-term revenue recognition and segment performance. In AMD's case, the 2020 pandemic led Microsoft and Sony to overproduce Xbox/PS4/PS5 consoles, building excessive inventory. The subsequent announced inventory clearance strategy reduced semi-custom chip orders to AMD, creating a temporary headwind rather than a structural business failure.

![Figure: Console gaming market analysis and inventory cycle data](frame:periodic_0009.jpg)

### Valuation Methodology

Forward price-to-earnings ratios prove more relevant than current P/E when assessing whether a stock is fundamentally sound. The instructor notes: "Even current P/E does not matter that much if the forward P/E is kind of sensible." This approach accounts for temporary earnings disruptions that may distort current metrics while maintaining focus on future earning power.

![Figure: Valuation metrics comparison showing forward vs. trailing P/E](frame:scene_0004.jpg)

The use of trailing twelve months (TTM) data provides more current performance assessment, while segment-level analysis helps distinguish between temporary inventory cycles and permanent demand destruction. Any segment showing greater than 20% year-over-year change requires root cause analysis to determine whether the movement represents cyclical inventory issues or structural market share loss.

![Figure: TTM financial performance data and trend analysis](frame:periodic_0010.jpg)

### Technical Pattern Recognition

Channel trading represents a tactical approach for timing entries, allowing investors to buy within support and resistance bands rather than waiting for full trend confirmation. This method enables earlier entry than waiting for complete trend reversal but requires prior fundamental conviction to justify the risk.

![Figure: Channel trading pattern with support and resistance levels](frame:scene_0005.jpg)

For trend reversal identification, the slope must change first before the overall trend can shift. However, as the instructor cautions: "Technicals do not work perfectly." This limitation reinforces why technical analysis should only be applied after establishing fundamental confidence in the business.

![Figure: Trend reversal signals and slope change indicators](frame:scene_0007.jpg)

The philosophy draws inspiration from Warren Buffett's approach: "Warren Buffett does not look at trends. He does not sit and trade channels. He just says that, okay, this stock is good. I am going to buy it." This perspective emphasizes the primacy of business quality over short-term price movements.

![Figure: Investment philosophy comparison between technical and fundamental approaches](frame:periodic_0017.jpg)

### Risk Management Through Business Quality

The core investment rule states: "Unless you see the value of the stock going up over time, right, so the value of the business becoming more valuable with time, you don't enter into any kind of stock." This principle ensures that all positions are grounded in fundamental business improvement rather than speculative price movements.

![Figure: Business quality assessment framework and criteria](frame:periodic_0018.jpg)

Revenue and profit generation serve as fundamental criteria for business quality. As emphasized: "If the business is not generating profits and generating revenues and increasing revenue, it's a useless business." This standard eliminates companies that lack sustainable business models regardless of their technical chart patterns.

![Figure: Revenue and profit trend analysis for business evaluation](frame:scene_0008.jpg)

The integration approach recognizes that different stock categories require different management strategies. High-growth stocks like Roblox and Palantir have less diversified revenue streams and higher volatility, requiring deeper fundamental analysis and more frequent monitoring. In contrast, stable businesses like Meta, Microsoft, and Amazon offer diversified revenue streams with more predictable cash flows, allowing for longer holding periods.

![Figure: Stock categorization by growth profile and risk characteristics](frame:periodic_0019.jpg)

### Data Sources and Research Methods

The US market provides exceptional transparency for fundamental analysis, with clean quarterly earnings reports and SEC filings readily available. The research process involves searching for company earnings reports, accessing official SEC filings, and reviewing key sections including revenue trends, profit metrics, segment-level breakdowns, and management commentary on business headwinds and tailwinds.

![Figure: SEC filing example showing earnings report structure](frame:scene_0009.jpg)

This data quality advantage enables thorough fundamental analysis that forms the foundation for investment decisions. The instructor notes: "The US market is very transparent in the sense that you can pretty much just read the reports and you can do further analysis. The data is clean."

![Figure: Earnings report analysis showing key financial metrics](frame:scene_0013.jpg)

The comprehensive approach requires integrating technicals, fundamentals, and macro analysis rather than relying on any single methodology. This integration demands significant time investment but provides the conviction necessary to hold positions through market volatility while maintaining the flexibility to time entries and exits based on technical signals.

![Figure: Integrated analysis framework combining multiple methodologies](frame:scene_0014.jpg)

The methodology emphasizes avoiding pure technical trading, as trends can reverse before positions are built, while fundamentals provide the conviction necessary to hold through volatility. This balanced approach enables investors to capture both the strategic value of quality businesses and the tactical advantages of well-timed entries and exits.

## Position Building & Margin of Safety

Systematic capital deployment represents a cornerstone of professional hedge fund management, requiring a disciplined approach to building positions over time rather than investing lump sums. This methodology centers on margin of safety calculations and staged position building to manage downside risk while maintaining upside capture potential.

![Figure: Portfolio construction timeline showing staged deployment](frame:scene_0107.jpg)

The fundamental principle underlying position building is that deployment methodology matters more than stock selection. As the instructor emphasizes: "It's not about which stock you're buying. It's about how you're buying it." This philosophy recognizes that even excellent stock picks can result in poor outcomes if position sizing and timing are mismanaged.

![Figure: Position sizing framework and capital allocation strategy](frame:periodic_0123.jpg)

### Margin of Safety Framework

The margin of safety concept combines external market conditions with internal portfolio positioning to create a comprehensive risk buffer. External margin of safety represents the percentage decline from market all-time highs, providing an objective measure of market discount. The instructor notes: "The easiest way of figuring out margin of safety" equals the percentage decline from all-time high.

![Figure: Margin of safety calculation methodology and examples](frame:scene_0108.jpg)

Internal margin of safety consists of unrealized gains built into existing positions, which adds to the external market margin of safety. For example, if an investor has 2.69% unrealized gains on deployed capital combined with a 3% market discount from peak, the total margin of safety equals 5.69%. The target is to maintain approximately 5-6% margin of safety across the portfolio.

![Figure: Internal vs external margin of safety components](frame:scene_0109.jpg)

When margin of safety expands beyond 10% due to market corrections, this creates opportunities to deploy additional capital. Conversely, when margin of safety contracts below 5%, new deployments should be reduced to maintain the protective buffer.

![Figure: Margin of safety thresholds and deployment triggers](frame:scene_0111.jpg)

### Staged Deployment Strategy

For a $1 million target portfolio with a 1-2 year deployment horizon, the systematic approach begins with an initial tranche of 20% ($200,000) when the market provides minimal margin of safety. This conservative initial deployment protects against immediate adverse moves while establishing market exposure.

![Figure: Initial deployment strategy for large portfolios](frame:scene_0115.jpg)

The phased approach continues with building internal margin of safety through unrealized gains on the initial investment. Once the combined internal and external margin of safety reaches the target 5-7% range, the portfolio is positioned for the next phase of deployment.

![Figure: Phase-based capital deployment over time](frame:periodic_0134.jpg)

If markets fall an additional 5-10% within six months, the margin of safety expands to approximately 10%, triggering deployment of additional capital ranging from $100,000 to $300,000. This deployment brings the margin of safety back down to the target 5-6% range, preventing the portfolio from being "naked" or fully exposed without downside protection.

![Figure: Opportunistic capital deployment during market corrections](frame:scene_0117.jpg)

### Technical Confirmation for Fundamentally Sound Stocks

The integration of technical analysis with fundamental conviction becomes particularly important for stocks in downtrends. AMD serves as an exemplary case study, where fundamental analysis reveals that inventory buildup represents a temporary cyclical issue rather than permanent market share loss.

![Figure: AMD technical analysis showing downtrend pattern](frame:scene_0119.jpg)

The technical entry strategy involves waiting for confirmation before buying fundamentally sound stocks in downtrends. Channel and trend line analysis helps identify optimal entry points, particularly when price touches or breaks below the central trend line within a downtrend channel.

![Figure: Channel trading strategy for downtrending stocks](frame:periodic_0140.jpg)

The key insight is that companies haven't "killed their relationship with AMD" but are simply absorbing existing inventory before ordering new stock. This distinction between temporary inventory cycles and permanent demand destruction is crucial for position building decisions.

![Figure: Inventory cycle analysis and relationship assessment](frame:scene_0123.jpg)

### Risk Management Through Position Scaling

The continuous scaling process involves moving from $200,000 to $500,000 to $1 million over the 1-2 year timeframe, with each addition timed to maintain the 5-6% margin of safety threshold. If markets rise without correction, purchases should be made at technical support levels within the uptrend.

![Figure: Scaling methodology and risk management protocols](frame:periodic_0143.jpg)

This approach prevents the common mistake of deploying all capital when markets are near highs. The instructor warns: "I have to be like a mad person to go and invest a crazy amount of money into US stocks right now in one go." Even with $1 million to deploy and bullish conviction, immediate full deployment when markets are near peaks represents reckless risk management.

![Figure: Risk management principles for large capital deployment](frame:periodic_0144.jpg)

### Market Cycle Recognition

Understanding market cycles enables better timing of position building phases. The instructor notes: "If you buy the market in a bear category, you will almost never lose money. Temporarily loss over, but over a two to three year period you will not [lose money]." A 20-21% market correction creates conditions where buying becomes nearly risk-free over a 2-3 year horizon.

![Figure: Market cycle identification and timing strategies](frame:scene_0129.jpg)

This principle justifies the staged deployment approach rather than lump-sum investing, allowing investors to capture lower prices during corrections while maintaining downside protection through margin of safety management.

![Figure: Bear market buying opportunities and risk assessment](frame:periodic_0147.jpg)

### Practical Implementation Examples

The methodology can be illustrated through specific market scenarios. When NASDAQ trades 2.5-3% from peak, this provides minimal margin of safety and suggests conservative deployment. When NASDAQ trades 14% from peak, this provides substantial margin of safety and supports more aggressive deployment.

![Figure: NASDAQ market scenarios and deployment decisions](frame:scene_0130.jpg)

The calculation mechanics involve tracking both unrealized gains on deployed capital and external market discounts. For instance, with $200,000 invested showing 2.69% unrealized gains and a 3% market discount, the total margin of safety equals 5.69%, which falls within the target range.

![Figure: Margin of safety calculation examples and scenarios](frame:scene_0131.jpg)

### Advanced Position Building Techniques

As portfolios grow and market conditions change, the position building strategy must adapt to maintain effectiveness. The approach emphasizes maintaining flexibility to respond to changing market conditions while adhering to disciplined risk management principles.

![Figure: Advanced position building for experienced investors](frame:scene_0132.jpg)

The framework recognizes that different market environments require different approaches. During periods of high volatility, smaller position sizes and more frequent rebalancing may be appropriate. During stable markets, larger positions with less frequent adjustments may be optimal.

![Figure: Market environment adaptation for position building](frame:periodic_0156.jpg)

### Integration with Fundamental Analysis

Position building must be grounded in fundamental conviction about business quality and future value creation. The technical timing serves to optimize entry points, but the fundamental thesis provides the conviction necessary to hold positions through inevitable volatility.

![Figure: Fundamental conviction as foundation for position building](frame:scene_0133.jpg)

This integration ensures that position building serves the broader investment strategy rather than becoming an end in itself. The goal remains building wealth through ownership of appreciating businesses, with position building methodology serving as the vehicle for optimal capital deployment.

## Key Takeaways

• **Fundamental analysis must precede technical analysis** - Business value assessment forms the foundation; technical timing serves as a tactical overlay for entry and exit optimization

• **Margin of safety is non-negotiable** - Maintain 5-7% combined internal and external margin of safety through systematic position building and market timing

• **Staged deployment beats lump-sum investing** - Deploy capital in tranches (20% initial, then scale based on margin of safety) rather than investing all capital at once

• **Segment-level analysis reveals true business drivers** - Understanding revenue by business unit helps distinguish temporary inventory cycles from permanent demand destruction

• **Position sizing discipline prevents concentration risk** - Maximum 5% allocation per stock for most investors, with absolute ceiling of 10% even for employee stock plans

• **Technical confirmation enhances fundamental conviction** - Use moving averages, channel analysis, and trend reversals to optimize entry timing on fundamentally sound stocks

• **Market cycles create deployment opportunities** - 20-21% market corrections provide nearly risk-free buying conditions over 2-3 year horizons

• **Quality businesses justify patient capital** - Focus on companies with sustainable competitive advantages, pricing power, and growing revenues rather than speculative plays

• **Risk management through diversification** - Maintain 20-30% international exposure to reduce single-country risk and currency depreciation impact

• **Valuation drives decisions, not purchase price** - Base hold/sell decisions on current valuation assessment rather than historical entry prices or arbitrary profit targets
