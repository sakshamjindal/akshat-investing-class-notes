## Portfolio Hedging Strategies and Cash-to-Investment Ratios

The foundation of portfolio protection begins with understanding two primary methods to safeguard mature portfolios from market corrections. When significant profits have been realized and capital preservation becomes the primary objective, investors must choose between defensive positioning and more sophisticated hedging approaches.

![Figure: Portfolio dashboard showing diversified holdings and performance metrics](frame:scene_0001.jpg)

The cash-to-investment ratio strategy represents the most accessible form of portfolio hedging. This approach allocates the portfolio in a specific ratio format, such as an 80/20 split where $800,000 is invested and $200,000 is held in cash reserves for a $1 million portfolio. The cash component is maintained in risk-free instruments including fixed deposits, debt funds, or savings accounts.

![Figure: Instructor explaining cash reserve deployment strategy](frame:periodic_0002.jpg)

The trigger condition for deploying these cash reserves occurs when the market experiences a "deep correction" defined as a decline exceeding 20% from peak levels. This threshold marks the technical definition of a bear market. When such corrections occur, the reserved capital is deployed to average down the cost basis on existing positions, allowing investors to reinvest at lower valuations and improve portfolio returns.

![Figure: Discussion of market correction thresholds and deployment triggers](frame:scene_0002.jpg)

A practical example illustrates this strategy's effectiveness: if QQQ is trading at the 600 level and experiences a 25% correction falling to 450, an unhedged $1 million portfolio would decline to $750,000. However, with the 80/20 strategy, the $800,000 invested position would decline proportionally, but the $200,000 cash reserve remains available for redeployment at the depressed 450 level.

![Figure: Instructor demonstrating portfolio allocation concepts](frame:scene_0003.jpg)

The instructor emphasizes that this strategy solves the specific problem of deep correction scenarios: "What is the precise problem I'm trying to solve? See, I'm trying to solve the precise problem that in case there is a deep correction, okay? Deep correction. And I'm able to deploy this 200K cash and bring the averaging cost down, right?"

![Figure: Explanation of averaging down mechanics during market corrections](frame:scene_0005.jpg)

This approach provides several advantages: it maintains dry powder for market dislocations, protects against forced selling during downturns, and allows opportunistic buying at depressed prices. The strategy transforms market volatility from a threat into an opportunity for improving long-term returns.
