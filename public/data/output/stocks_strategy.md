## 1. Investment Philosophy and Core Principles

### 1.1 Fundamental vs Technical Integration Framework

The cornerstone of this investment philosophy rests on a **fundamental-first, technical-second approach** where business value assessment must precede all technical analysis. This hierarchy is non-negotiable and forms the foundation of all investment decisions.

#### The Core Principle

> "If you don't see these businesses becoming more valuable with time, then don't do any trend analysis."

This statement encapsulates the entire philosophy: technical analysis serves as a tactical tool for entry and exit timing, not as a strategic decision-maker. The integration methodology combines fundamental analysis for stock selection with technical analysis for optimal timing.

#### Integration Methodology

| Analysis Type | Primary Function | Decision Level | Time Horizon |
|--------------|------------------|----------------|--------------|
| Fundamental Analysis | Stock selection and thesis development | Strategic | 3-5+ years |
| Technical Analysis | Entry/exit timing optimization | Tactical | Days to months |
| Macro Analysis | Market context and cycle positioning | Contextual | 1-3 years |

#### Practical Application Framework

**Step 1: Business Quality Assessment**
- Evaluate revenue generation and growth trajectory
- Assess profit creation and margin sustainability
- Determine value enhancement over time
- Identify competitive advantages and moats

**Step 2: Technical Timing Integration**
- Only after fundamental conviction is established
- Use support/resistance levels for entry optimization
- Apply moving average systems for trend confirmation
- Implement channel trading for position building

**Step 3: Continuous Validation**
- Monitor fundamental thesis against quarterly results
- Adjust technical parameters based on volatility changes
- Reassess macro context for portfolio positioning

#### Market Reality Acknowledgment

The framework explicitly recognizes that trends can reverse rapidly before meaningful positions are built, making fundamental conviction essential. Without underlying business understanding, technical analysis cannot provide reliable answers. As stated in the source material:

> "Business analysis is the hardest part and where most money is made."

This multi-disciplinary requirement demands proficiency in technical analysis (timing), fundamental analysis (valuation), and macro analysis (context) working in concert.

### 1.2 Margin of Safety and Entry Point Philosophy

The margin of safety framework serves as the foundation of hedge fund management, emphasizing the acquisition of assets at discounted prices rather than market peaks.

#### Core Philosophy

> "Money is not really made at the time of chasing the trends. Money is made if you buy things which are at a discount."

This principle drives all capital deployment decisions and creates the mathematical foundation for superior long-term returns.

#### The 20% Correction Trigger

The framework establishes a **mechanical entry signal** when markets decline 20% from recent highs:

| Correction Level | Classification | Action Required |
|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| <10% | Minor Correction | No major portfolio adjustments |
| 10-20% | Major Correction | Systematic buying opportunities |
| >20% | Deep Correction | Aggressive deployment with potential leverage |

**Historical Validation:**
- 2007-2009 NASDAQ declined 53% from peak to trough, followed by approximately 100% recovery
- 2020 COVID crash provided exceptional entry points with subsequent 100%+ recoveries

#### Three-Phase Strategy Framework

**Phase 1: Entry Point Identification**
- Critical principle: "If we find a great point of entry on a portfolio, we have won the war"
- Deploy 80-85% of capital at identified market bottoms
- Requires patience—optimal conditions may not appear for months or years
- Bulk investing approach captures full recovery from optimal price levels

**Phase 2: Momentum Riding**
- Active monitoring with periodic profit-taking to build cash reserves
- Withstand normal 12-18% drawdowns within uptrends
- Distinguish between temporary volatility and genuine trend reversal
- Volatility is "the inherent cost of equity market participation"

**Phase 3: Profit Realization**
- Convert notional gains to realized profits through strategic exits
- Withdraw 100% of gains to transform into "real profits"
- Lock in performance while maintaining core positions
- Timeline between stages varies from 3 months to 5 years

#### Mathematical Framework for Downward Averaging

The core mathematical principle demonstrates asymmetric returns:

| Scenario | Price Movement | Return Calculation |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Asset declines | 100 → 80 | -20% loss |
| Buying at discount | 80 → 100 | +25% gain |
| Net advantage | — | 5% additional return |

**Compounding Effect Example:**
- Starting position: 100 units
- Experience 25% loss: 75 units
- Capture 104% recovery: 150-160 units final value

This mathematical outcome demonstrates why systematic buying during corrections generates substantial outperformance versus buy-and-hold strategies.

This framework distinguishes between genuine growth investments and speculative plays, providing clear criteria for portfolio allocation decisions.

**Real Growth Minimum Return:**
> Inflation + 3% = Minimum acceptable return

| Inflation Rate | Minimum Required Return | Rationale |
|---------------|------------------------|-----------|

The fundamental question for any investment:

> "Does the world become exponentially better due to the asset's existence?"

**Assets That Pass the Test:**

**Assets That Fail the Test:**

| Asset | Reason for Failure |
|-------|-------------------|

The framework prioritizes genuine productivity contributors over speculative assets, even when short-term returns favor speculation:

| Investment Category | Allocation Priority | Rationale |
|--------------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Productivity Contributors | Primary (60-75%) | Sustainable long-term value creation |
| Speculative Plays | Limited (10-15%) | Asymmetric opportunity capture |

#### Narrative vs. Fundamentals Distinction

**Current Phase (2020-2030):**

- Creates opportunities in genuine growth assets
- Dangerous overvaluation in speculative plays

**Historical Precedent:**
- 2000-2010: Emerging markets narrative generated 5x returns
- 2010-2020: Mature narrative produced lower 2x returns
- Narrative shifts drive multi-year return patterns

**Investment Approach:**
- Understand the "why" behind growth, not just price momentum
- Distinguish sustainable productivity advantages from temporary narratives
- Align with technological paradigm shifts (AI, autonomous vehicles)

## 3. Fundamental Analysis Methodology

### 3.1 Segment-Level Revenue Analysis Framework

The deep-dive methodology requires analyzing each revenue segment separately to identify growth drivers and headwinds that may be obscured in consolidated financials.

#### Core Methodology

**YoY Growth Tracking Requirements:**
- Monitor year-over-year growth rates by business unit
- Track quarterly progression for trend identification
- Compare segment performance against company guidance

**Anomaly Investigation Rule:**
> Any segment showing >20% YoY change requires root cause analysis

This threshold triggers mandatory investigation to determine whether changes are temporary (cyclical) or permanent (structural).

#### AMD Case Study: Segment Analysis in Practice

| Segment | YoY Growth | Assessment | Action Required |
|---------|-----------|------------|-----------------|
| Data Center | +69% | Strong growth driver | Monitor for sustainability |
| Gaming | -59% | Major headwind | Root cause analysis required |

**Root Cause Analysis for Gaming Decline:**
- Microsoft/Sony console inventory clearance (temporary)
- 2020 pandemic led to overproduction
- Inventory cycle, not structural demand destruction
- Conclusion: Temporary headwind, not business failure

#### Distinguishing Temporary vs. Structural Issues

| Indicator | Temporary Issue | Structural Issue |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|
| Cause | Inventory cycles, one-time events | Market share loss, obsolescence |
| Duration | 2-4 quarters | Ongoing deterioration |
| Management Commentary | Acknowledges and provides timeline | Defensive or evasive |
| Competitive Position | Unchanged | Deteriorating |
| Customer Relationships | Intact | Terminating |

**Key Distinction:**
> "Distinguish between inventory absorption and relationship termination"

Inventory cycles represent temporary earnings disruptions, while relationship terminations indicate permanent business deterioration.

#### Research Process Requirements

**Data Sources:**
- SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q)
- Quarterly earnings reports
- Official company communications
- Management conference calls

**Key Sections to Analyze:**
- Revenue trends by segment
- Profit metrics by business line
- Segment breakdowns with YoY comparisons
- Management commentary on outlook

**Transparency Advantage:**
> "The US market is very transparent in the sense that you can pretty much just read the reports and you can do further analysis. The data is clean."

### 3.2 Valuation Methodology and Forward P/E Priority

The valuation framework prioritizes forward-looking metrics over historical multiples, recognizing that growth companies require different assessment approaches.

#### Forward P/E Priority Principle

> "Even current P/E does not matter that much if the forward P/E is kind of sensible."

This principle acknowledges that trailing metrics may not reflect future earnings power, particularly for companies in growth or turnaround phases.

#### TTM Data Usage

**Trailing Twelve Months Application:**
- Provides current performance assessment
- More relevant than annual data for fast-changing businesses
- Use segment impact to distinguish temporary vs. permanent issues

#### Tech Company Valuation Context

|-----------|------------|--------|
| 30-40 | Reasonable for high-growth tech | Standard allocation |
| 40-60 | Elevated but acceptable with growth | Reduced allocation |
| 60-90 | Requires exceptional growth justification | Minimal allocation |
| 90-160+ | Overvaluation threshold | Avoid or reduce |

## 4. Technical Analysis and Market Timing

### 4.1 Three Moving Average Trend-Following System

The primary technical analysis system uses three simple moving averages to identify trends and generate trading signals.

#### System Configuration

| Moving Average | Color Code | Function |
|---------------|------------|----------|
| 50-day SMA | Black | Fast/lighter indicator |
| 150-day SMA | Blue | Slower/heavier indicator |
| 200-day SMA | Green | Long-term reference |

**Platform:** TradingView.com recommended for implementation

#### Uptrend Alignment Rules

**Bullish Configuration:**
- 50-day above 150-day above 200-day
- All three lines sloping upward
- "Black on top, then blue, then green, and all of them should be changing"

**Physical Metaphor:**
> Faster MA "floats up" while slower MA "comes down" during trend changes

#### Trend Reversal Signals

**Reversal Identification:**
- Simultaneous slope changes in both 50-day and 150-day MAs
- Transition from 200>150>50 (downtrend) to 50>150>200 (uptrend)
- Marks beginning of new trend cycles

**Current Signal Example (QQQ):**
- 50-day crossing below 150-day confirms downtrend
- Creates buying opportunity for systematic accumulation

#### Data Quality Requirements

| Market | Data Quality | Strategy Reliability |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|
| US Markets | "Great news is that this data is quite solid" | High reliability |
| Indian Markets | "In India, this data is not solid" | Low reliability |

**US Market Advantages:**
- Clean trend-following signals
- Reliable system performance
- Deeper liquidity and institutional participation
- Transparent price discovery mechanisms

**Indian Market Challenges:**
- Excessive noise and false signals
- Frequent slope changes without corresponding trend reversals
- Strategy unreliable due to data quality issues
- Requires additional filters or alternative strategies

#### Position Management During Crashes

**Parabolic Drop Strategy:**
> "You do nothing. If you have more money, you invest."

**Rationale:**
- Markets can fall 5-7 days without warning
- Attempting exits during panic selling proves ineffective
- Use crashes for opportunistic buying

**Historical Example (2020 Crash):**
- Parabolic drop over 5-7 days
- 44-45% gain within six months of bottom
- Demonstrates crash timing difficulty and recovery opportunity

### 4.2 Support and Resistance Channel Trading

The channel trading strategy enables earlier entry than waiting for complete trend reversal, requiring prior fundamental conviction.

#### Fundamental Trading Rule

> "Buy at support levels, never at resistance."

**Common Mistake:**
> "Buying them on uptrend. Buying them late on uptrend."
